* **2015 DR370**: a = 416 au, q = 40.2 au, i = 16.1°, ϖ = 186°, U = 8. Rubin (X05) recovered it in 2026 and the MPC linked it to the 2015 DECam (W84) discovery: arc 2015-02-17 to 2026-05-13, 101 observations. JPL still carries the 5-day 2015 orbit, so it fails the arc cut there. A genuine new member.
* **2025 LS2**: a = 530 au, q = 43.1 au, i = 12.6°, ϖ = 336°, Rubin discovery. The real arc is 350 days (MPC `get-orb`). `Distant.txt` writes it as "2025-2026", which the pipeline used to read as 365.25 d and so admitted it. This is now fixed: the arc cut uses `arc_days_min`, the shortest arc the year span and last-observation date allow, plus exact arcs from `data/raw/mpc_exact_arcs.csv`. 2025 LS2 now fails the cut by 15 days, as it should. It should pass after its next observation. The fix does not change the paper's three samples (checked by a new test).

**N_bb16 = 32.**

Short-arc candidates beyond the cut: 21 objects with a > 250, q > 30 have orbits but arcs under a year (`2026-11-05_new_a250_candidates.csv`). Fifteen of them are 2025–26 Rubin discoveries with arcs of 13–120 days (U = 6–9), the rest are old single-opposition orbits. The largest Rubin orbit is 2025 MC403 (a = 864 au, 67-d arc), probably one of the "~1,000 au" objects in the 2 Nov news summaries. 16 Rubin-discovered a > 250 au objects in total (with 2025 LS2).

## Evidence change from the new objects

Same likelihood, priors, selection cube and nested sampler as `infer_planet9.py` (nlive = 500). The 31-object run reproduces `nondet.json` exactly (ln K = 0.52).

| sample | ln K (orbits + non-detection) | ln K (orbits only) |
|---|---|---|
| paper, 31 | 0.52 ± 0.10 | 0.90 ± 0.10 |
| + 2015 DR370 (32) | 0.12 ± 0.10 | 0.52 ± 0.10 |
| + 2025 LS2 (33) | 0.20 ± 0.10 | 0.69 ± 0.10 |

2015 DR370 lies about 60° from the inferred planet direction and far from the cluster centre, so it pulls the evidence down, by Δ ln K = −0.40. This is below the |Δ ln K| > 1 needed for an update note. Raw numbers: `2026-11-05_delta_lnK.json`.

## Literature and announcements

* MPECs: the only distant objects were 2026-U85 (2026 ED138, a = 43 au, classical belt) and 2026-U86 (a = 71 au, e = 0.51, scattered disk). No ETNO discovery circulars.
* arXiv: three papers on 2026 ED138 (2610.38693, 2610.40106, 2611.02371), a Rubin Neptune Trojan paper (2610.38309), and LiDO (2610.19865), a characterised high-inclination survey with no objects beyond 63 au. No Planet Nine detection claim, no new clustering analysis, no Sheppard–Trujillo or DES characterisation.
* Rubin DP2: Early DP2 was released 27 July 2026; full DP2 is expected December 2026. It includes **SSObject** (299,343 rows), **SSSource**, an MPC orbit snapshot, and a **Visit** table (28,698 visits, April 2025 – January 2026). The Solar System products are associations with objects known by 13 March 2026, including Rubin discoveries reported to the MPC by then. There is no standalone Rubin discovery catalogue (promised for later data releases). Access needs Rubin data rights.
